Prologue

The Archaeology
of Stillness

Text is not an inert imprint upon paper. When unburdened by haste, words breathe with the porous grain of linen, hovering in the fragile interval between matter and vapor.

As you move through these pages, allow your glance to slow. Each line gathers density only while resting within your direct focus; as your gaze drifts onward, the charcoal ink yields to the canvas, unraveling into wisps of quiet air.

Chapter I

Where Words Fall Silent

Some mornings in the studio on Lake Zurich, I do not pick up a brush for hours. I sit in the unhurried grey light as it moves across raw gesso. I listen to whatever is underneath the silence.

Starting is not a rational decree—it is a surrender. The mineral pigment arrives before the intention; the feeling precedes the contour. And then the hand responds, setting into motion an encounter that was never planned.

“Stillness is never empty—it is full of unseen movement.” — Shalona

We mistake quietude for absence, yet it is the precise density from which all genuine form condenses. When a candle is snuffed out, the flame does not vanish; it transforms into a curling ribbon of smoke that inscribes the air with its memory.

Chapter II

Colour as Memory and Geology

I have always felt pigment in the body before comprehending it with the intellect. Raw ochre announces itself when something ancient wishes to speak. Deep Prussian blue is not sorrow—it is oceanic depth, the courage to descend past the reach of surface reflections.

When I work with lake water washes and photographic transfers, the image begins to break down. The chemical certainty of the photograph dissolves, leaving only a ghost of texture that mingles with raw mineral stone.

To paint is to witness a material lose its boundaries until it begins to speak as atmosphere.

Chapter III

The Honest Grace of the Unfinished

For many seasons I concealed the unfinished works—the canvases turned face-to-wall, the compositions abandoned mid-breath. I believed incomplete meant defective.

Now I recognize that certain pieces are not abandoned; they are simply waiting. They hold a question I have not yet earned the right to answer, or an emotion I have not yet lived all the way through.

“In the unfinished, there is no artifice. You see the exact boundary where the artist chose humility over force.” — Studio Notes, 2026

An open question speaks with far greater intimacy than a closed conclusion. The space left unpainted is the very space in which the viewer is invited to reside.

Chapter IV

On You, Standing There

I perpetually hold in mind the person who will one day pause before these layered canvases. I do not know your name. I do not know what grief you carry, or what quiet hope guides your steps.

Yet I trust that if work is born of genuine stillness, something inside the paint will find you. Not my narrative, but yours. A painting becomes whole only when someone stands before it and brings their own silence into communion with the mark.

Float through the mystical. In the dispersing smoke, discover your own empowerment.
